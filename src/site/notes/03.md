## Activity 10 — Mushawrat Process (Detail)

In Islam, meetings and gatherings are an important aspect of community, communication, and decision-making. There are certain principles and etiquettes that Muslims generally follow when conducting meetings.

Here is a 10 step process & guidelines that you will explain to shababs in detail.

1. **Intention (Niyyah):** Just like any other action in Islam, it's recommended to begin with a sincere intention. Before starting the meeting, participants should remind themselves of the intention behind the gathering which should always be "ALLAH KI RAZAA", whether it's for seeking knowledge, making a decision, solving a problem, or any other positive purpose.
2. **Invocation of Blessings (Dua):** It's common to start the meeting with a supplication (dua) seeking blessings, guidance, and wisdom from Allah. This can help set a positive tone for the meeting and invoke Allah's assistance in the proceedings.
3. **Greeting (Salam):** Begin the meeting with the Islamic greeting of peace, "Assalamu Alaikum" (peace be upon you). This promotes a sense of unity and respect among participants. Our Prophet S.A.W always started his conversations with Salam.
4. **Orderly Conduct & Roles in Mashawrat:** Meetings should be conducted in an orderly and respectful manner. Appoint Ameer before the start of mashwarat who will lead the whole process. One person will be given the responsibility to write meeting notes, conclusions & Assigned tasks. One person will be given responsibility to make ZIKR during the meeting to seek Allah's blessings during the meeting. Zikr is a powerful tool which helps keep Satan away from us. Participants should listen attentively when others are speaking and avoid interrupting.
5. **Consultation (Shura):** Consultation is an important concept in Islam. When making decisions, it's encouraged to involve others and seek their opinions and advice. The concept of "Shura" (consultation) is derived from the Quran and the Sunnah (traditions of the Prophet Muhammad). Even though our Prophet S.A.W is the wisest man of this entire world still he would take suggestions from his companions. Many times he made his decisions based on the suggestions of his companions.
6. **Moderation and Balance:** Avoid excessive length or overly detailed discussions that might lead to fatigue or a loss of focus & waste the time of all attendees.
7. **Respect for Time:** Islam places value on time management. Meetings should start and end on time out of respect for the participants' schedules and commitments.
8. **Decision-Making:** If the purpose of the meeting involves making decisions, consult the Quran, Sunnah, and Islamic principles to ensure that the decisions align with the values of the faith. Decisions should be made after careful consideration and consultation. Once the decision has been made every participant should have firm faith and trust in Allah that the decision made is the right one because we followed the Quran and Sunnah during our mashwara.
9. **Conclusion with Dua:** Just as the meeting began with a supplication, it's a good practice to conclude with a dua, asking for Allah's guidance, acceptance, and blessings for the outcomes of the meeting. You may also add the below Sunnah dua at the conclusion of mashwara.
10. **Gratitude:** Express gratitude to Allah for the opportunity to gather, communicate, and engage in productive discussions. Being thankful is an essential aspect of Islamic faith.

Remember that the specific customs and practices may vary among different Muslim communities and cultures, but the general principles of respect, consultation, good manners, and seeking Allah's guidance remain constant in the context of meetings/Mashwarat.
