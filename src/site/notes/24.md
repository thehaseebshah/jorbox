# Journaling and Mindfulness

## Learning Objectives

Cultivating sincere self-accountability (Muhasabah) by reflecting on one's deeds, instilling a conscious awareness of death (Dhikr al-Mawt) to focus the heart on the Akhirah, fostering a state of gratitude (Shukr) and repentance (Tawbah), and establishing journaling (Tadwin) as a practical tool for spiritual growth.

## Activity 1: Journaling and Mindfulness

### Instructions

This is a deeply personal and serious activity. The Murabbi must establish a quiet, sincere, and respectful atmosphere, free from distractions.

#### Part 1: The Session of Tafakkur (Deep Reflection) — Duration: 10 minutes

**Setting the Stage:** The Murabbi will ask all Shabab to sit comfortably, straighten their backs, and close their eyes. He will instruct them to block out all distractions and focus inwardly for the next 10 minutes.

**Phase A — The Life Review (5 minutes):**

The Murabbi will guide the Shabab with a calm voice to:

- "Think back on your life. Mentally list the major blessings Allah has bestowed upon you—your health, your family, your Iman. Feel the gratitude (Shukr) for these gifts."
- "Now, with honesty, think about your sins. The major ones and the small ones you've forgotten. Feel the weight of these actions and the need for sincere repentance (Tawbah)."

**Phase B — The Certainty of Death (5 minutes):**

The Murabbi will then shift the focus:

- "Imagine your life is over. Picture your own body being washed and shrouded. See yourself being lowered into the darkness of the grave, alone."
- "Contemplate this reality. This is the ultimate end of all worldly pursuits. The goal of this reflection is to detach your heart from the dunya and connect it to the reality of the akhirah."

#### Part 2: The Pen of Accountability (Tadwin) — Duration: 25 minutes

**The Murabbi's Counsel (10 minutes):**

After the reflection, the Murabbi will explain the importance of journaling as a tool for Muhasabah. He will explain:

- *Why we write:* "Great scholars of the past kept journals not for memories, but for accountability. Writing down your thoughts makes them real. It turns a fleeting feeling of guilt or gratitude into a concrete plan for action. Your journal is a record of your Jihad al-Nafs (struggle against the self)."
- *What to write:* "This is between you and Allah. No one will read it. Be brutally honest." The Murabbi can then suggest some prompts to guide their writing:
  - What is one blessing from today you were truly grateful for?
  - What is one sin you committed that you need to repent for sincerely?
  - If you knew you had one week left to live, what is one thing you would change immediately?
  - What is one concrete commitment you can make to be a better Muslim tomorrow?

**The Journal Entry (15 minutes):**

The Shabab will spend the next 15 minutes writing their private journal entry based on the reflections and prompts. The Murabbi must ensure there is absolute silence during this time. The activity concludes when the time is up. There is no sharing session, as the purpose is private accountability.
