### Munazra (The Scholar's Blade)

#### Murabbi's Briefing:

Before the activity, the Murabbi will gather the Shabab and deliver a foundational talk.
This is not a game; it is the revival of a noble Islamic tradition.
(10 minutes)

#### Key Talking Points:

##### What is Munazara?

Explain the Islamic science of debate and disputation.
Its goal is not to "win" for the sake of ego, but to collaboratively arrive at the truth (Haqq).

Contrast this sharply with the soulless, Western style of debate, which is often about sophistry, manipulation, and winning at any cost, even if it means obscuring the truth.
Our tradition is rooted in sincerity (Ikhlas).

##### A Tradition of Giants:

Remind them that our greatest scholars, like Imam Abu Hanifa and Imam al-Ghazali, were masters of Munazara.
They used it to clarify complex issues and defend the Deen from deviant ideologies.
This is the heritage of strong, confident, intellectual Muslim men.

##### The Rules of Engagement (Adab al-Bahth):

Introduce the core ethics of Islamic debate.
This is what separates our men from boys and our scholars from shouting heads on TV.

- Attack the argument, not the man.
- Listen respectfully without interrupting.
- Define your terms clearly.
- Acknowledge a valid point made by your opponent.
- The intention must be to seek truth, not to humiliate your brother.

##### Relevance Today:

In an age of doubt, social media mobs, and relentless attacks on Islam, the ability to think clearly and articulate a strong, well-reasoned argument is a form of Jihad with the tongue and pen.
This skill is essential for future leaders, fathers, and defenders of the Ummah.

#### Setup

- **Form the Groups:** Divide the Shabab into groups of 4.
- **Assign Squads & Stances:** Within each group, create two squads of two.
  The Murabbi gives the group a topic card.
  One squad is assigned the "For" (Ithbāt / Affirmation) stance, and the other is assigned the "Against" (Nafy / Negation) stance.

##### Provide Example Topics:

Topics should be debatable and relevant.
For example:

- "For the sake of the Ummah's unity, individual freedom of expression must be curtailed."
- "Technological advancement (AI, social media) is a greater threat to the Iman of the youth than a benefit."

#### Debate Flow

##### Phase 1: Preparation (30 mins):

The two squads in each group work separately.
They must prepare their arguments, anticipate the other side's points, and decide who will deliver each part of the debate.

##### Phase 2: The Munazara (20 mins per debate):

One group debates at a time in front of the others.
All Shabab groups present at once in parallel.
The Murabbis act as the moderators, audience and timekeepers.
The flow is strict:

- **Opening Statement (For):** 2 mins
- **Opening Statement (Against):** 2 mins
- **Rebuttal 1 (For):** 2 mins
- **Rebuttal 1 (Against):** 2 mins
- **Rebuttal 2 (For):** 2 mins
- **Rebuttal 2 (Against):** 2 mins
- **Closing Statement (Against):** 2 mins
- **Closing Statement (For):** 2 mins

##### Phase 3: The Verdict & Debrief:

After the debate, the Murabbi scores both squads and declares a winner.
More importantly, he gives a debrief, praising examples of strong arguments and adherence to Adab, and correcting any major flaws in reasoning for the benefit of all listeners.

**Language of Debate:** Urdu

#### Evaluation & Scoring

The Murabbi will score each squad out of 20 points.
The victory goes to the team with the higher score, not necessarily the team whose stance was "correct."

| Criterion | Description | Points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strength of Argument (Hujjah) | Quality of logic, reasoning, and evidence used. Was the case compelling? | / 5 |
| Rebuttal & Takedown | How effectively did the squad dismantle their opponent's arguments and expose weaknesses? | / 5 |
| Adherence to Adab | Did the squad maintain Islamic etiquette? (Respect, no interruptions, focus on ideas). | / 5 |
| Clarity & Delivery (Bayan) | How clearly, confidently, and persuasively did they present their points? | / 5 |
| Total Score |  | / 20 |
