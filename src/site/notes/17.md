## Activity 1: Public Speaking Tips
Watch [this](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Iqq1roF4C8s) first and let Shabab watch it in the park.

Then read and understand the following stratedgies Muhammad Qahtani used in this winning talk, and teach them to Shabab while breakind down the video:

Mohammed Qahtani’s award-winning Toastmasters speech, "The Power of Words," is widely considered a masterclass in public speaking.

By analyzing his delivery and structure, we can map out the exact public speaking techniques and tricks he used to win the World Championship:

### 1. The Subversive "Hook" (Visual Incongruity)

Qahtani opens his speech by stepping up to the microphone, pulling out a cigarette, and putting a lighter to it.

- The Trick: This completely shatters expectations. In a formal public speaking championship, doing something seemingly prohibited or shocking forces the audience to immediately snap to attention.
    
- The Follow-Up: After letting the tension sit for a second, he drops the punchline: "What? Or do you all think smoking kills?". He hooks the room instantly with curiosity.
    

### 2. Strategic Use of Humor to Build Trust

Throughout the first half of the speech, he feeds the audience "alternative facts" (e.g., that tobacco farming is the real reason for the war on smoking, or that sugar is three times more deadly).

- The Trick: He uses a deadpan, authoritative delivery to make a ridiculous claim, only to pivot and reveal that he made it all up just to prove how easily words can manipulate people. This self-aware humor immediately wins over the room.
    

### 3. Immersive "Play-Acting" and Stage Movement

Qahtani doesn’t just tell stories; he acts them out, shifting his physical location on stage to mark a transition into a new narrative space.

- The Trick: When talking about his 4-year-old son drawing on the wall, he physically kneels on the stage floor to simulate talking down to a child.
    
- Vocal Sound Effects: During a critical, emotional story about a friend in the hospital, he mimics the audio environment, literally making the "beep, beep, beeeeeep" sound of a flatlining heart monitor and yelling "Clear!" to mimic a defibrillator.
    

### 4. Flawless Emotional Pivots (Humor to Pathos)

Great speeches do not stay on one emotional frequency. Qahtani expertly takes the audience on an emotional rollercoaster.

- The Trick: He transitions seamlessly from lighthearted, laughing scenarios (his son, the fake science about cigarettes) to a devastating, highly personal climax about a friend dying because of cold, unsupportive words from a father. This contrast makes the heavy, emotional moments land much harder.
    

### 5. Rhetorical Repetition (Anaphora)

As he approaches the emotional peak of his message, he uses rhythmic, structural repetition to drum the point into the audience's minds:

- The Trick: He states: "Words have power. Words are power. Words could be your power." Repeating the structure at the beginning of successive sentences binds the core takeaway together, giving it a poetic, anthemic weight.
    

### 6. The "Call to Action" and Full-Circle Close

A classic speech rule is to tie the ending back to the beginning to provide the audience with a sense of closure.

- The Trick: He brings back the cigarette prop at the very end to anchor his final message. He wraps up by giving the audience an inspiring, actionable choice: "Your mouth can spit venom, or it can mend a broken soul. Ladies and gentlemen, let that be our goal."

## Activity 2: Bol keh Lab Azaad hen Tere

### Setup

Groups Formation: Divide Shabab into groups of 10. If numbers don't divide evenly, groups of 8-12 are acceptable.

Speaking Order: Each group sits in a circle. Number the participants 1-10 to establish speaking order.

Topic Pool: Prepare 15-20 topic cards with funny, desi-themed topics (see topic list below).

Timer Setup: Have a visible timer or stopwatch ready for precise timing.

Audience Arrangement: Other groups sit as audience to listen and provide supportive feedback.

### How to Play

#### Round Structure:

- Each speaker gets exactly 2 minutes to speak
    
- Each speaker gets exactly 2 minutes to prepare
    
- Preparation and speaking happen simultaneously (parallel processing)
    

#### The Process:

Step 1 (Topic Assignment): Speaker #1 draws a random topic card and begins their 2-minute preparation immediately.

Step 2 (Parallel Preparation): As Speaker #1 starts speaking, Speaker #2 immediately draws their topic card and begins preparing.

Step 3 (Seamless Transition): When Speaker #1's 2 minutes are up, Speaker #2 immediately begins speaking while Speaker #3 draws their topic and starts preparing.

Step 4 (Continuous Flow): This pattern continues until all 10 speakers have completed their turns.

#### Speaking Rules:

- Must speak for the full 2 minutes (no stopping early)
    
- Must stay on topic throughout
    
- Can use humor, personal anecdotes, or creative storytelling
    
- Must be respectful and appropriate
    
- Urdu/English mix is encouraged for authentic desi flavor
    

#### Audience Engagement:

- Audience must remain silent during speeches
    
- Supportive reactions (clapping, nodding) are encouraged
    
- No heckling or interruptions allowed
    

#### Winning & Scoring

Judging Criteria (Murabbi evaluation):

- Fluency: How smoothly did they speak without long pauses?
    
- Creativity: How original and engaging was their content?
    
- Humor: Did they successfully incorporate funny elements?
    
- Cultural Touch: How well did they use desi references?
    
- Time Management: Did they utilize the full 2 minutes effectively?
    

Scoring System:

- Each speaker receives a score from 1-5 in each category
    
- Maximum possible score: 25 points
    
- Winner is the highest-scoring speaker from all groups
    

Recognition:

- Top 3 speakers receive verbal recognition
    
- Most creative speaker gets special mention
    
- Funniest speaker gets audience choice award

### Rules & Strategy

Preparation Strategy:

- First 30 seconds: Understand the topic and choose your angle
    
- Next 60 seconds: Think of 2-3 main points or stories
    
- Final 30 seconds: Plan your opening and closing lines
    

Speaking Strategy:

- Start with a hook or funny observation
    
- Use personal experiences when possible
    
- Include desi phrases and references naturally
    
- End with a memorable conclusion or joke
