## Activity 1 — Natural Direction Finding

Navigation is a critical skill for outdoor adventures, survival situations, and fostering self-reliance. It enhances your confidence and reduces vulnerability to technological failures. Several natural methods can be used to find your direction.

### 1. Celestial Navigation

This technique involves using celestial bodies like the sun, moon, and stars to find your way.

**Using the Sun:**

- *Solar Noon:* The sun is at its highest point in the sky at roughly solar noon, indicating South in the Northern Hemisphere and North in the Southern Hemisphere.
- *Shadow Method:* Place a stick upright in the ground and mark the tip of its shadow. After 15-30 minutes, mark the new position of the shadow's tip. A line drawn between these two marks points from west (the first mark) to east (the second mark).
- *Watch Method:* In the Northern Hemisphere, point the hour hand of an analog watch towards the sun. The line that bisects the angle between the hour hand and the 12 o'clock mark will point South.

**Using the Stars (at Night):**

- *Polaris (The North Star):* The North Star is a reliable indicator of North as it is positioned almost directly above the Earth's North Pole. *Finding Polaris:* Locate the Big Dipper constellation. Identify the two "Pointer Stars" that form the outer edge of the Dipper's bowl. Extend an imaginary line from these stars, and it will lead you directly to Polaris. The altitude of the North Star above the horizon corresponds to your latitude.
- *Using the Moon:* In the Northern Hemisphere, the illuminated side of a crescent moon points roughly towards the west (sunset direction).
- *Using Planets:* Bright planets like Venus and Jupiter, often visible after sunset or before sunrise, can help determine direction based on their position.

### 2. Other Natural Methods

- *Natural Markers:* Moss often grows more abundantly on the north side of trees in the Northern Hemisphere.
- *Landmarks:* Use distinct features like mountains or rivers for orientation.
- *Animal Behaviour:* Some animals travel along established paths which can be used for direction.
- *Wind Patterns:* A prevailing wind that consistently blows from one direction can help with orientation.

## Activity 2 — Solar Oven Challenge

Building a solar oven is a practical activity that demonstrates how to harness the sun's power to cook food using simple materials. The oven's efficiency will depend on factors like sunlight intensity, reflector angle, and insulation.

### Instructions for Building

1. **Prepare the Box:** Choose a suitable cardboard box; a pizza box is recommended due to its flap lid. Paint the inside of the box black with spray paint or line it with black construction paper to help absorb heat.
2. **Create a Reflective Surface:** Cover the inside of the box lid securely with aluminum foil, ensuring it is smooth to reflect sunlight effectively.
3. **Make a Clear Cover:** Cut a piece of clear plastic wrap or an oven bag to cover the box's opening. This creates a greenhouse effect, allowing sunlight in while trapping heat.
4. **Enhance Heat Absorption:** Line the bottom of the box's interior with black construction paper or paint it black. This dark surface is crucial for absorbing and retaining heat.
5. **Assemble and Seal:** Attach the reflective lid to the box. Seal any gaps around the edges with tape to minimize heat loss.
6. **Adjust and Test:** Use a ruler or stick to prop the reflective lid open at an angle to direct the maximum amount of sunlight into the box. Once the oven is placed in direct sunlight and has heated up, you can test it by cooking simple items like small vegetables or eggs.

## Activity 3 — Water Filtration

This activity provides hands-on learning about the importance of clean water and how natural materials can be used to filter contaminants. It encourages critical thinking about the efficiency of different filtration methods.

### Instructions for Building a Filter

1. **Introduction:** Begin with a discussion on the importance of clean water for humans and ecosystems. Highlight how this is a good survival skill.
2. **Setup the Funnel:** Cut the bottom off a plastic bottle to create a funnel. Place this funnel upside down into the top of a second, intact bottle which will collect the clean water.
3. **Layer the Filtration Materials:** First, place a coffee filter or a piece of fine cloth at the neck of the funnel bottle to prevent the filtering materials from falling through. Assemble the layers inside the funnel in the following order from bottom to top:
   - Gravel or small stones (to filter out larger debris)
   - A layer of sand (for finer particles)
   - A layer of activated charcoal (for absorbing impurities)
   - A final layer of sand
4. **The Filtering Process:** Slowly pour the dirty water into the top of the filtration system. Observe how the water drips through the layers and collects in the bottom bottle.
5. **Discussion:** After filtering, discuss the visible changes in the water's clarity and the significance of water purification. Reflect on how to contribute to water conservation and pollution prevention.

💧 (You don't have to use 3 bottles, you can do this with just one bottle – and use tissue instead of cloth.)

## Activity 4 — Igniting Fire with Natural Methods

Starting a fire without modern tools is a fundamental survival skill that teaches perseverance, problem-solving, and resourcefulness. This challenging activity builds patience (sabr) and reliance on effort. Always prioritize safety and be aware of local fire regulations.

### 1. Gather Your Materials

Before attempting any method, you must gather three types of materials:

- **Tinder:** Highly flammable material that ignites with a spark, such as dry leaves, grass, or fine wood shavings.
- **Kindling:** Small sticks and twigs that will catch fire from the tinder.
- **Fuel:** Larger pieces of wood to sustain the fire once it is established.

### 2. Fire-Starting Methods

- **Fire by Friction:** These methods generate heat by rubbing wood together.
  - *Bow Drill:* A spindle is rotated at high speed against a dry hearth board using a bow, creating friction dust (an ember) that can be transferred to tinder.
  - *Hand Drill:* Similar to the bow drill, but the spindle is rotated rapidly between the palms of your hands.
  - *Fire Plough:* Rub a dry piece of wood back and forth along a groove carved into another flat piece of wood to generate friction and heat.
- **Flint and Steel:** Strike a piece of high-carbon steel against a sharp edge of flint to create sparks. These sparks must be caught on tinder, like char cloth, to create an ember.
- **Solar Fire:** Use a lens (magnifying glass, piece of clear ice, bottom of a soda can) to focus sunlight onto a single point on a tinder bundle. This method requires patience and bright, direct sunlight.
- **Other Methods:** Advanced techniques include creating an electric arc from a portable power source or using chemical reactions, such as mixing potassium permanganate and glycerin.
